# Smear Frames and Duplicate Limbs (guide Kvon shared)

**Fact or rule:** On November 18 Kvon pasted a summary of Sir Wade Neistadt's video on animating high-speed motion and asked me to refine the animations with it. Its points:
- **Duplicate limbs:** when something moves too fast to track between frames, show several of its poses within the same few frames. Ways: rig controls that toggle extra limbs; a referenced copy of the character with everything but the limb hidden, animated along the arc; extracted mesh copies (cheap, no rig); simple shapes or 2D image planes with a surface (unlit) shader.
- **Smear frames:** short, distorted frames between extreme poses to sell speed without motion blur. Ways: stretch / scale / translate rig controls then return them; lattice or nonlinear (bend, flare, twist) deformers, lattices driven by keyed clusters; camera-view lattice scripts; simple 3D / 2D shapes with a surface shader standing in for the smeared form.
- **Pro-tip:** don't start with these - block the motion first, add smears / duplicates last, only where readability needs them.

**How it maps here (my note):** the game already uses the "simple shapes" smear method - the Keyblade smear layers (make_smears.py) and the grab effects (make_grab_fx.py). To find where more is needed, measure each animation: how much of the silhouette changes between drawings, divided by how many frames the drawing is shown (scratch script, November 18). Top result: the air grab's held opponent (85-94% change per 2-frame drawing through both spins and the throw). **Applied:** fighter.gd GHOST_* - a held fighter's last two drawings stay as fading (42% / 20%), cool-tinted afterimages where they were drawn (duplicate limbs; ground and air grab; in game, no re-render). Not applied (lower need): Standing Medium's swing (91% in 2 frames, but it already has a Keyblade smear), the jump takeoff (three 1-frame drawings - meant to read as instant).

**Why:** Kvon shared the guide to refine the animations; no further reason given.

**Weapon smear guide (Kvon, November 18, second guide: bat-swing smears - elongated in-between, multiples, elongated multiples "often the most effective for general combat", the Alan Becker shape-shifted style, crescent / anticipation smears), "try this for the weapon swings and use your other suggestions for the movement of Sora's body":**
- **Keyblade elongated multiples + crescent:** `blender_workflow/make_key_multiples.py` (run with no arguments; --debug writes overlays to combat/boxes_preview/keymult_*.png). For each swing's hit drawing (SWINGS: the 8 attacks except the Standing Light bash) it cuts the Keyblade's own pixels from the game drawing (silver / gold in a corridor around the blade projected from the 3D report's key_track - same projection as make_boxes.py, checked to line up) and copies them back along the swing: 3 copies at 40 / 66 / 88% of the swing's last part (at most 70 deg behind the blade - a big swing fanned out like a comb on the first try), each 11 thin sub-copies (= stretched along the motion), tinted toward the smear blue, 26 / 40 / 58% opacity, UNDER the existing crescent. On the drawing before the hit of the heavies (Standing / Crouching / Jumping Heavy) a thin, sharp anticipation crescent along the first half of the coming arc. It writes into the game's smear frames (KingdomArena/assets/sora/attack_*_smear_384/); the first run saved the originals to `<2d version>/frames_smear_384_base/` and every run starts from those (safe to re-run).
- **Body squash and stretch:** fighter.gd DASH_STRETCH - the air dash's burst (jump_dash drawing 0: x1.22, y0.88) and the back-dash's hop (back_dash drawing 1, 17 px / frame: x1.18, y0.9; its first drawing, the push, is slow), anchored at the feet, visual only (hurtboxes unchanged).
- **Sora's own afterimages** through the air grab's two spins (GHOST_SPIN_DRAWINGS 3-18), same trail as the held opponent. Busier, as expected - tune GHOST_ALPHA if it is too much.
- Not used: the Alan Becker style (frame-by-frame redrawn shapes - would mean redrawing frames, not this pipeline).
